Building at the Intersection of Climate+Tech

The Stanford Cleantech Hackathon brings together an interdisciplinary group of students to build tangible solutions grounded in real Stanford climate research — from localized adaptation and public health to grid reliability and flood resilience. Teams of 2–5 hack from 5 PM Friday to 5 PM Saturday in the Mackenzie Room at the Jen-Hsun Huang Engineering Center.

Tracks

🌎 Climate Intelligence

How can we turn climate research and Earth observation into actionable, local intelligence?

Challenges:

1. Localized adaptation evidence assistant Source: Burke, "A Unified Global Platform for Scaling Effective Adaptation" Build a tool that takes a place and a hazard (e.g. "Fresno County, extreme heat") and returns what the research actually says works there, sourced and localized.
2. Detecting mosquito breeding sites from aerial imagery Source: Rosser, Mordecai, LaBeaud et al., 2026 Planetary Intelligence cohort Use computer vision on drone or high-res aerial imagery to find discarded tires, containers, and trash piles that hold standing water, then map breeding hotspots for vector control teams.

🔋 Resilient Systems

How can we keep power on and communities safe as climate hazards intensify?

Challenges:

1. Predictive power outage and reliability mapping Source: Rajagopal, Majumdar & Cezar, "ReliAdapt" Combine utility outage records, weather, and infrastructure data to predict where outages will hit hardest, then recommend distributed energy resources for the most exposed users.
2. Participatory flood early warning for informal settlements Source: Wong-Parodi & Goldberg, 2026 Climate Adaptation cohort Build a two-way flood alert system for dense, data-sparse urban areas: it pushes warnings out and lets residents report conditions back in.

⚡ Energy Efficiency

How can we use less energy, waste less on the grid, and make every kilowatt-hour count?

Challenges:

1. Smart load shifting and demand response Build tools that move flexible loads (EV charging, HVAC, water heating, compute jobs) to hours when the grid is cleaner and less congested, cutting both costs and emissions.
2. Building and household energy insights Turn smart meter, sensor, or utility bill data into clear, personalized recommendations that help homes, campuses, and businesses find and fix energy waste.
3. Grid efficiency and integration Forecast demand and renewable generation, reduce transmission losses, or optimize how batteries and distributed resources plug into the grid.

FAQ

How does team formation work?
  • Team size: 2–5 people. We encourage students from different majors and backgrounds to team up, but it's not required.
  • Pre-formed teams are allowed, and there will be dedicated team-building sessions during the event.
Can I bring a previous project to the hackathon?

You are encouraged to think about ideas and approaches beforehand, but all development should be done during the hackathon (honor code!).

How will I receive updates during the event?

We will use a dedicated Slack workspace.

How does mentorship work?

There will be dedicated tables and Slack channels with experienced mentors for the two tracks. Mentors are available to brainstorm ideas, approaches, help with bug fixes, and more!

How do I submit a project, and when is it due?

Submit on Devpost by 5 PM Saturday, October 24.

Will food be provided?

Yes! Dinner on Oct 23, and breakfast, lunch, and dinner on Oct 24, with plenty of dietary options. For food accommodations, email angelali@stanford.edu before Monday, October 19, 8 AM.

Can I stay overnight at the venue?

No, there will be no overnight options available.

What's the WiFi?

You can use Stanford Visitor, Stanford, or Eduroam.

I have a question not answered here

Please email angelali@stanford.edu or sacha@raisable.ai and we will do our best to help!
